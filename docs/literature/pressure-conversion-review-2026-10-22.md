# Pressure-to-altitude methods in the introduction

Targeted source check, 22 October 2026. This note supports revision of the conversion-history paragraph. Local full-text methods were checked against publisher sources and a targeted online search. This is not a systematic estimate of method prevalence. No manuscript text or rendered documents were changed.

## Main findings

Reference-pressure strategy and temperature-profile assumptions are separate choices. Reanalysis can supply sea-level pressure alone, surface pressure and temperature at a known model elevation, or temperature information aloft. Behavioural calibration can be combined with reanalysis rather than replacing it.

A known-height event supplies a pressure–height anchor for estimating subsequent airborne heights as well as ground elevations. It does not determine the intervening temperature profile or eliminate weather changes between the anchor and the later measurement. Ground contact supplies an absolute anchor only if its elevation is known; height above a changing terrain surface additionally requires position and terrain elevation.

Fixed-standard conversion remains documented in recent work, including Brodersen et al. (2026). The checked examples support “continues to be used in recent geolocator studies”; they do not establish a numerical prevalence or that most studies use it.

## Implications for wording

Keep geolocator applications first: Meier, Rime, Dufour and Doniol-Valcroze demonstrate that reanalysis correction is already applied to miniature pressure records. Use Schaub as a further profile-refinement example and clearly identify the GPS-tag context. Station-based and reanalysis methods can be introduced as accounting for weather-related pressure changes rather than claiming a universal established accuracy advantage.

Explain Meier and ERA5 together: Meier replaces the fixed sea-level pressure while retaining a standard temperature profile; ERA5 surface methods additionally use local reference temperature and model elevation. Explain known-height anchoring in its own sentence, including that anchors still require a temperature-profile assumption and sufficiently representative pressure conditions for the later estimate.
